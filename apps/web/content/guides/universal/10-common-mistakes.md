Ten mistakes cause the overwhelming majority of estate disasters. Not exotic tax structures. Not complex trust failures. These ten. Every one of them is avoidable in a single afternoon.

## 1. Not having a will at all

Roughly half of North American adults die without one. Every state and province has an intestacy formula that decides who inherits — a formula that ignores your relationships, your intentions, and often your family structure. Unmarried partners typically inherit nothing. Judges pick guardians for minor children.

**The fix:** A basic will costs $50–$500 with an online service or $500–$2,500 with an attorney. Do it this week.

## 2. Beneficiary designations that don't match your will

Your will governs assets in your name at death. Retirement accounts, life insurance, and TOD/POD accounts pass by beneficiary designation and *override your will*. Ex-spouses, deceased parents, and forgotten names show up as still-named beneficiaries in roughly a third of estates.

**The fix:** Once a year, pull every designation. Update anything that's stale. See our [beneficiary designations guide](/guides/beneficiary-designations).

## 3. Naming a minor child as a direct beneficiary

Minors can't legally receive inheritances directly. When it happens anyway, a court appoints a conservator (US) or Public Guardian and Trustee (Canada) to manage the funds until age of majority — often with high fees and rigid rules.

**The fix:** Name a trust as the beneficiary, or use a UTMA custodianship (US) or in-trust-for account (Canada). Consult a lawyer for anything over $100k.

## 4. Not funding the trust you set up

Setting up a revocable living trust and not re-titling assets into it is the most expensive mistake we see with high-income planners. You pay for the trust but get none of the probate-avoidance benefit because your assets are still in your personal name.

**The fix:** Every asset you want in the trust needs a new deed, brokerage titling, or beneficiary form. Set aside a Saturday.

## 5. No power of attorney or healthcare directive

Wills only work after death. If you're incapacitated, someone still needs to manage your finances and make medical decisions. Without a POA and healthcare directive, your family needs a court order — expensive and slow.

**The fix:** Both documents can typically be signed in a lawyer's office in one visit for $200–$800 total. In some jurisdictions, valid free forms exist.

## 6. Leaving your executor with no information

Even a perfect estate plan fails if the executor can't find the assets, the accounts, or the professional advisors. Executors regularly spend 6–12 months just discovering what the deceased owned.

**The fix:** Keep a current asset inventory (see our [asset inventory checklist](/guides/asset-inventory-checklist)) and store it somewhere your executor can access. LegacyVault exists partly to solve this.

## 7. Not talking to family about the plan

The most common source of post-death family conflict is surprise. Adult children finding out about disparate inheritances, second spouses, or unusual bequests for the first time at the reading of the will. Most of it can be defused by a single conversation while you're alive.

**The fix:** See our [family conversation guide](/guides/family-conversation-guide).

## 8. Ignoring digital assets entirely

Bitcoin wallets, domain names, iCloud accounts holding decades of photos, business systems that require your login to keep running. Most estate plans say nothing about any of these.

**The fix:** See our [digital goodbye checklist](/guides/digital-goodbye-checklist) and use a credential vault (like the one built into this product) so your executor can access the digital half of your life.

## 9. Estate plan out of date

Marriage, divorce, birth of a child, death of a beneficiary, moving to a new state or province, buying real estate, starting a business — any of these can make your existing plan wrong. Plans written more than 10 years ago are almost always wrong in some material way.

**The fix:** Review annually. Update after any major life event.

## 10. Assuming "my family will figure it out"

They won't. Even loving families fracture under the stress of unclear expectations, uneven grief, and the practical demands of settling an estate. The average estate involves 12–18 institutions to notify, dozens of forms to file, and legal deadlines that don't wait for you to feel ready.

**The fix:** Do the work now, while you have the clarity, the time, and the energy. It's a gift to the people you love most.
