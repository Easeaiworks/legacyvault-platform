Every estate lawyer has the same story: months after distribution, someone finds an account, a policy, or a title deed that nobody knew existed. On average, 30% of what a person owned at death goes undiscovered — swept into unclaimed-property registries, forgotten institutions, or dead-drop safe deposit boxes.

The fix is a full asset inventory, written down while you're alive, updated once a year, and stored where the right person can find it. Not a spreadsheet you'll never look at again — a living document. Here's the complete list of what to include.

## Bank and cash-equivalent accounts

- Every chequing and savings account (including small ones you've stopped using)
- Certificates of deposit / GICs / term deposits — record the maturity date
- Money market accounts
- Foreign currency accounts, even if the balance is small
- Prepaid cards, gift cards with meaningful balances, PayPal / Venmo / Interac e-Transfer balances
- Cash on hand — safe, home safe, safe deposit box (list the box location and where the key is)

## Investment accounts

- Every brokerage account, including inactive ones
- Employer retirement plans — current and every former employer
- IRAs, Roth IRAs (US) / RRSPs, RRIFs, TFSAs, LIRAs, RESPs (Canada)
- Mutual fund holdings held directly with fund companies
- Individual stocks held in physical certificates (still exists for older holdings)
- Cryptocurrency wallets — exchange accounts AND self-custody wallets (list seed phrase location, not the phrase itself)
- Employee stock plans, ESOPs, RSUs, stock options — vested and unvested

## Real estate

- Every deeded property — primary residence, secondary, rental, vacant lot
- Time-shares (often forgotten, still trigger fees indefinitely)
- Mineral rights, water rights, royalty interests
- Real property held jointly, in a trust, or in a family LLC

## Insurance

- Life insurance — every policy, including small ones from your bank, employer group coverage, and any policies your parents may have taken out on you as a child
- Annuities
- Disability, long-term care, critical illness policies
- Property, auto, umbrella insurance policies

## Debts (someone has to pay these — or discharge them)

- Mortgages and HELOCs
- Personal loans, student loans, credit card balances
- Business debt personally guaranteed
- Tax debts
- Recurring subscriptions (Netflix, gym, streaming, software) — these keep charging your card

## Physical property

- Vehicles — car, boat, RV, motorcycle, ATV, aircraft (list VIN)
- Jewellery, art, collectibles, watches
- Firearms — critical to inventory because transfer rules are strict in most jurisdictions
- Home furnishings of significant value
- Safe deposit box — where it is, where the key is, who's authorized on the account

## Business interests

- Any private company shares — corporation, LLC, partnership
- Sole proprietorships (business assets and liabilities)
- Intellectual property — patents, trademarks, copyrighted works
- Royalty streams from books, music, licensing deals

## Digital and easily-forgotten

- Domain names you own
- Social media accounts with commercial value
- Online course businesses, e-commerce inventory
- Points and miles — airline, hotel, credit card rewards (some transfer at death, most don't)
- Digital photos and videos — where they live, how to access

## Death benefits and what your survivors are owed

- Social Security / Canada Pension Plan / Quebec Pension Plan survivor benefits
- Veterans' benefits (VA in the US, Veterans Affairs Canada)
- Employer group insurance and pension survivor benefits
- Provident funds, foreign pensions
- Union and professional association death benefits
- Credit card and mortgage life insurance (bundled products you may have forgotten)

## How to actually maintain this

Every year, on your birthday or on New Year's Day, take two hours. Update the inventory. Add anything new, remove anything sold. Put the date on it. Store one copy in your vault, one printed copy in a fireproof safe, and tell one trusted person where both live.

That two hours a year is the difference between your family finding everything you built for them and 30% of it going to the province.
